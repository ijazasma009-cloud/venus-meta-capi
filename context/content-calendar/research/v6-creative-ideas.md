### The Razor Receipt
**Carousel · props only**

What you see: Flat charcoal 4:5 field. One real thermal till receipt from any Lahore supermarket, crumpled once by hand, flattened, shot from directly above on the charcoal card under a window, rotated about four degrees. Everything on it is set in a typewriter monospace. Header, two centred lines: SHAVING, ITEMISED. Then line items left, figures right, aligned in one column: Razor, every third day ... 8 min. Re-shave the night before a shaadi ... 20 min. Stubble back by Thursday ... 0 min saved. Nick on the jaw ... 3 days. A blank line, then TOTAL in larger mono with 71 HOURS A YEAR right-aligned. A full row of asterisks as the tear line. A real barcode block. Under the barcode, where a shop address would go, one line: venusaesthetics. Nothing else on the canvas. No logo, no phone number, no colour except the charcoal and the paper. Slide two is the same receipt torn cleanly in half.

Why: The receipt is the joke and the information at the same time, so it reads in half a second and still rewards a stop. The right-hand column is minutes and days, not money, which keeps it inside the no-price rule and makes it far more uncomfortable than a rupee figure would be. It is a reference object, so people screenshot it. It needs no model, which solves the modesty problem outright, and it costs one receipt and one afternoon.

### Cross Section, Razor and Laser
**Single graphic · design only**

What you see: Cream ground, no photography anywhere. One hairline vertical rule in the accent colour runs down the exact centre, stopping short of the top and bottom margins. Two column heads in a high-contrast serif: Razor on the left, Laser on the right. Under each head sits the same flat vector cross-section of skin drawn in three flat colours only, a pale skin block, a pink dermis band, a black hair shaft. Left version: the shaft sliced flush at the surface, follicle still packed and dark. Right version: the shaft lifted clear, follicle empty and pale. Below the diagrams, three short paragraphs per column in a grey sans, baseline-aligned across the gutter so the eye reads them as three pairs, with a large gap between each pair. The handle sits alone in the accent colour at the bottom centre. No logo lockup, no CTA, no numbers.

Why: This is the single clearest way to say why laser is not the same as shaving, and it says it with a picture rather than a claim. The two diagrams are identical except for one detail, so the difference is the whole message. Row alignment across the gutter means you can read only the left column or only the right and it still makes sense. Pure vector, so the designer builds it in an afternoon and it never needs a patient, a model or a consent form.

### SAME BULB, SAME WATTAGE
**Single graphic · props only**

What you see: Two identical clear filament bulbs in two matching white lamp holders, side by side on a plain dark grey table, shot straight on in a dim room. Both switched on. The left bulb has been smeared with a trace of oil and dusted with talcum powder so the glass is filmed over and its light is muddy and diffuse. The right bulb is clean and its filament is sharp and bright. Nothing else in frame. Words bottom left under the dull one: SAME BULB. Words bottom right under the clean one: SAME WATTAGE. Second beat, white text on black: DULL IS NOT DARK. DULL IS COVERED. If you want it as a Reel, just wipe the left bulb clean on camera in one stroke.

Props: Two identical clear filament bulbs, two matching lamp holders or a twin socket, talcum powder, a smear of cooking oil, a dark grey board or cloth for the surface. Hardware shop plus supermarket, under an hour.

Why: It separates dullness from skin colour, which is the single most important move a Pakistani clinic can make in this category without sliding into whitening. Nobody argues that the dusty bulb is a worse bulb. The light output is the argument, not the glass. Reads in half a second, no face, no model, and the two thirds male half of the audience will accept a bulb frame long before they accept a skincare frame.

### Ten Minutes Longer
**Single graphic · props only**

What you see: Two flatbreads side by side on a steel tray on white. The left one pale gold and fresh from the tandoor. The right one the same bread left in too long, dark and blistered at the edges. Nothing between them but one line: Same dough. Ten minutes longer.

Why: It reframes sun damage as cooking time rather than as a lecture about UV, which is the only version of that message anyone will stop for. It is funny, it is food, and it is the most shareable image on this list. Buy two breads and ask the tandoor to burn one.

### Your Car Is Not Shade
**Single graphic · design only**

What you see: A flat skin toned card with a dense cluster of brown pigmentation spots airbrushed onto it. The cluster stops at a hard straight edge in the exact shape of a car window and door frame, so the spots sit only where the glass would be. A steering wheel rim enters the bottom of the frame. ### Bald Eggs
**Single graphic · props only**

What you see: An open carton of six eggs on a wooden table, each with a tired male face drawn on in black marker. Five are bare. The sixth has a small tuft of fresh coriander pushed through a pinhole in the top so it reads as a full head of hair. Shot straight on at table height, soft window light.

The one egg with greenery is the hope, so the post is not a joke about baldness, it is a joke with an exit. Eggs, a marker and a bunch of coriander.

### A Patch Of Lawn
**Single graphic · quick phone shoot**

What you see: Phone held directly above a small square of park grass. The left half is thick and green, the right half is bare brown soil, with a straight clean dividing line down the middle so the whole thing reads as a widening parting. A garden sprinkler head is pushed into the bare half, pointing up.

Why: From directly above, grass and a scalp are the same picture, and the straight line makes the brain see a parting before it sees a lawn. The sprinkler stands in for feeding the scalp rather than covering it up, which is the honest version of the PRP story. Ten minutes in any park.

### Every Seed Lands

### One Clean Arc
**Reel · quick phone shoot**

### One Pass
**Story · props only**

What you see: Vertical frame for Story. A tarnished brass drinking glass standing on newspaper. A hand rubs one narrow vertical stripe with a cloth and that stripe comes up mirror bright while everything around it stays dull brown. Ends on the half and half glass. One tap of text: One pass.

Why: The single bright stripe against the tarnish is a before and after inside one object, which is far more convincing than two photos side by side and impossible to accuse of editing. Built vertical and wordless so it works as a Story with a poll or a swipe up. A brass glass and a cloth.
